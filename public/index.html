Second.Source chooseyourbias.com

Article-level analysis of how Florida politics gets covered.

Not whether a story is true. Who got quoted, how far down the opposing view appears, which claims trace back to a document, and what one outlet left out that the rest of the coverage carried.

The one rule that matters

The scorer never sees who wrote the article. Outlet name, masthead references, byline, author bio, source URL and boilerplate are stripped before a single word reaches the model. A score describes the text in front of it, not the reputation attached to it.

Published output is counts the code computed and verbatim quotes with links back to the original. Nothing is republished here, and no summary is written on a publication’s behalf.

  • Wire anchor. Copy from the News Service of Florida is republished across the state and functions as the zero point. If it doesn’t score near-neutral, the engine is wrong, not the wire.
  • Known-lean separation. Outlets should separate in the directions predicted in advance, with symmetric magnitude. Asymmetric magnitude means lean inherited from the model rather than found in the coverage.
  • Valence swap. Flip party names and actor identities so the structure is identical and the direction reverses. A structural engine returns the same score.
  • Test–retest. Every article scored five times, with the variance published rather than hidden.

Results of all four will be published before any coverage analysis is, including the ones that come back badly. The predictions are written down first so they can’t be adjusted to fit what comes out.

What this will not do

  • Fact-check claims. This measures how a story was reported, not whether it was right.
  • Score opinion columns or editorials.
  • Republish anyone’s work. Scores link out.
  • Produce a single composite number before the tests above pass.
  • Let a human approve a score before it publishes.
