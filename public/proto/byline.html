Four outlets covered the same committee vote. Three quoted the sponsor and nobody else.

The vote was 9–4. Getting to the four took one phone call, and one newsroom made it.

Second Source/ August 14, 2026/ 1,400 words/ This is about the coverage, not about the bill.

SB 1104 cleared its first committee on Tuesday by a margin of 9–4. Four of the outlets tracked here published a story on it within eighteen hours. All four got the vote count right. All four correctly described what the bill does. On the facts, there is nothing to complain about.

What separates them is who got to talk. In three of the four stories, the only person quoted on the substance of the objection was someone who supports the bill, characterising the objection. The four members who voted no are named in every story. One outlet called one of them.

1 of 4 stories quoted a member who voted against. All four named them.

The phone call nobody made

Only the wire story reached a member who voted no. That story ran 940 words, shorter than two of the three that didn’t, which disposes of the idea that this was a space problem.

“Every exemption sounds narrow on the day it passes.” Rep. Daniel Okonkwo, quoted in Florida’s Voice, ¶14 of 22

Florida’s Voice did quote him — nine words, in the fourteenth paragraph of a twenty-two paragraph story, after the case for the bill had been made four times. Nine words is not nothing. It is also not an account of why four people voted no.

The specific objection raised in committee was that the exemption text reaches aggregate rate data, not only individual meter records. That distinction appears in three of the four stories as a line of bill description. It appears in none of them as something a person said, which is the form in which a reader would notice it mattered.

“Voted no” means a member who cast a no vote was quoted, not merely named. Florida’s Voice quoted one at ¶14; the wire quoted one at ¶4.

What would have fixed it

One call. The four no votes are a matter of public record, their offices publish phone numbers, and the wire service reached one of them on the same deadline. This is the cheapest correction available in political reporting and it is the one most reliably skipped.

Worth saying plainly: none of these stories is wrong, and the outlet with the widest quote gap here is not the outlet with the worst sourcing this month. Those are different measurements and they are published separately for that reason.
